PRACTICAL 1

School Adoption Program

The image presents a project poster for the School Adoption Program (School Adoption Plan), built around the idea "Adopt • Support • Empower". It outlines a social entrepreneurship concept developed under the Superior University, CMACED, SEP and Active Citizens programs, with the message "Better Schools, Brighter Futures."

Core Concept and Problem Statement

  • The Challenge: Many local schools suffer from poor infrastructure and facilities, a shortage of resources, and limited access to quality education.
  • The Solution: Adopt a school and improve its facilities, provide teaching support and educational resources, and organise educational programs for students.

Implementation and Target Audience

  • Target Community: Local schools in need of support, especially underprivileged schools with poor infrastructure and limited resources.
  • Beneficiaries: Approximately 100 to 500+ students.
  • Action Plan (8 Weeks):
    • Weeks 1–2 – Assessment & Planning: Survey the school's needs and create a detailed plan.
    • Weeks 3–4 – Support & Improvement: Improve infrastructure such as classrooms, electricity and furniture.
    • Weeks 5–6 – Education Drive: Conduct teaching sessions, skill workshops and awareness programs.
    • Weeks 7–8 – Review & Impact Check: Evaluate progress and ensure long-term support.

Infrastructure Improvements

  • Upgrade School Facilities: Classrooms, library, sanitation and more.
  • Provide Teaching Support: Qualified educators and educational resources.

Benefits and Expected Impact

  • Benefits: Quality education for every child, a better future for the community, and skill development and confidence in students.
  • Expected Impact: Improved learning environment, higher academic performance, increased school enrolment, and a brighter future for children.

Economic Model and Long-Term Vision

  • Estimated Project Cost: PKR 50,000 – 100,000.
  • Long-Term Vision: A well-equipped, high-performing school; an educated and empowered community; and a more equal and skilled society.

Alignment with UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs)

  • SDG 4 (Quality Education): Improves access to quality education through better facilities and teaching support.
  • SDG 17 (Partnerships for the Goals): Builds partnerships between the university, community and schools to achieve shared goals.

STOMACH

The stomach is a J-shaped, hollow, muscular organ located in the upper left part of your abdomen, just below the diaphragm. It sits between the esophagus and the small intestine. Its main role in the digestive system is to store food, churn it, and begin the digestion of proteins.

Anatomy and Location

  • Location: It lies in the left upper abdomen (epigastric and left hypochondriac regions), partly covered by the rib cage and the left lobe of the liver. When moderately full it holds about 1 litre, and it can stretch to hold much more after a large meal.
  • Structure: The stomach is divided into four main regions:
    • Cardia: The small area around the opening where the esophagus enters the stomach.
    • Fundus: The rounded, dome-shaped upper part that sits above the cardia and often holds swallowed air.
    • Body: The largest, central section where most of the churning and mixing happens.
    • Pylorus: The funnel-shaped lower part (antrum and pyloric canal) that leads into the duodenum.
  • Curvatures: The short, concave right border is the lesser curvature, and the long, convex left border is the greater curvature.
  • Sphincters: The lower esophageal sphincter guards the entrance, and the pyloric sphincter controls the exit into the small intestine.

Layers of the Stomach Wall

LayerFunction
MucosaInnermost layer with gastric glands and folds called rugae; secretes mucus, acid and enzymes.
SubmucosaConnective tissue carrying blood vessels, lymphatics and nerves.
Muscularis externaThree muscle layers (oblique, circular, longitudinal) that churn and mix food.
SerosaThin outer covering that reduces friction with nearby organs.

How It Works in Digestion

  1. Reception and Storage: Swallowed food enters through the lower esophageal sphincter. The stomach relaxes and stretches to hold it.
  2. Mechanical Digestion: Strong muscle contractions (peristaltic waves) churn the food and mix it with gastric juice into a thick, semi-liquid mixture called chyme.
  3. Chemical Digestion: Parietal cells release hydrochloric acid (HCl), which creates a strongly acidic environment and kills most bacteria. Chief cells release pepsinogen, which the acid converts into pepsin, an enzyme that starts breaking down proteins.
  4. Protection: Mucous cells produce a thick layer of mucus so the stomach does not digest itself.
  5. Emptying: The pyloric sphincter releases small amounts of chyme into the duodenum, where digestion and absorption continue.

Relevance in Medical Imaging

  • Barium Meal (Upper GI Series): The patient drinks a contrast agent and X-ray or fluoroscopy images show the stomach outline, rugae and emptying.
  • CT Scan: Used to examine the stomach wall, nearby organs and complications in the abdomen.
  • Ultrasound and MRI: Help assess surrounding structures and some stomach conditions without radiation (MRI/ultrasound).
  • Common Conditions Evaluated: Gastritis, peptic ulcers, hiatus hernia, gastric tumours and delayed gastric emptying.
Summary: The stomach stores food, mixes it with acid and enzymes, begins protein digestion, and passes chyme to the small intestine in a controlled way.
